# Delivering the Impossible

<div class="split" markdown="1">
<div markdown="1">

<p class="lede">Most software projects don't fail because of bad code. They fail because of a bad way of seeing the project in the first place.</p>

Every project is really a bet, and it happens in a swamp: a mess of stakeholders, hidden constraints and competing interests that no plan drawn up in a meeting room can anticipate. The way through is research. Get working software in front of real users early, even when it's embarrassing, and treat bad news as data.

## Ideas in the book

<ul class="phrases">
<li><strong>Bad news is data.</strong> Treat it that way, instead of as an attack.</li>
<li><strong>All projects are swamps.</strong> You can't plan your way across ground you haven't explored.</li>
<li><strong>The project is a bet.</strong> Break one big bet into many small ones.</li>
<li><strong>Point of view is worth 80 IQ points.</strong> How you see the project matters more than the tools you use.</li>
<li><strong>Deliver the possible, not the fantasy.</strong></li>
</ul>
